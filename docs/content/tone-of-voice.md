# Tone of voice

How BPM sounds on the website. The voice stays the same everywhere. The tone changes with the reader's situation.

## Why this voice

Most competitor sites sound corporate, old-fashioned or generic. BPM's readers are often volunteer directors and worried leaseholders, not property professionals. Public reviews of BPM are poor, so broad promises will not be believed. The voice has to earn trust with clear, specific, useful words.

## The voice

| We are                                                                         | We are not                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Plain:** short sentences, everyday words, legal terms explained              | Legalistic, full of jargon                              |
| **Specific:** facts, numbers, steps, names                                     | Vague ("comprehensive solutions", "unrivalled service") |
| **Steady:** calm and confident, like an experienced manager                    | Boastful, salesy, dramatic                              |
| **Helpful:** we answer the question the reader came with                       | Self-centred, all about BPM                             |
| **Straight:** we say what is included, what costs extra, and what happens next | Evasive, or hiding detail in small print                |

## Principles

### 1. Say the useful thing first

Put the answer, the action or the main point at the start of the page, the section and the sentence.

- **Instead of:** "Today, everyone is all too well aware of the emphasis placed on 'Rights and Responsibilities'."
- **Write:** "As an RMC director, you have legal duties. We help you meet them."

### 2. Be specific, not grand

Replace an adjective with a fact. If there is no fact, cut the sentence.

- **Instead of:** "Uncompromising Property Protection: Safeguarding your assets with strategic oversight."
- **Write:** "Regular inspections: your property manager visits the building and checks the communal areas."

### 3. Talk about the reader, not about us

Use "you" more than "we". Describe the reader's problem and what changes for them.

- **Instead of:** "We began with a simple truth. Property management is not about buildings. It is about people."
- **Write:** "You get one dedicated property manager, available Monday to Friday in office hours."

### 4. Explain the law in plain English

Many readers meet terms like Section 20, RTM or service charge for the first time. Explain a term the first time it appears on a page if the page's main reader may not know it. Use the wording in `glossary.md`.

Do not explain terms the main reader uses every day, such as "RMC" on a page for RMC directors or "freeholder" on a page for freeholders. Keep the intro for the reader's problem. If a definition would push the main point out of the intro, give it later on the page, in an FAQ, or link to the page that explains it.

- **Instead of:** "Section 20 consultation will be undertaken in accordance with statutory requirements."
- **Write:** "If major works will cost any leaseholder more than £250, the law says your freeholder, RMC or RTM company must consult you first. This is called Section 20 consultation. We run that process for them."

### 5. No metaphors or flourishes

Say what BPM does in literal words.

- **Instead of:** "We are the silent guardians ensuring its narrative remains strong."
- **Write:** "We do the day-to-day work, so the board can focus on decisions."

## Writing rules

### Grammar and style

- **Spelling:** UK English (organise, programme, licence as a noun, license as a verb).
- **Person:** "we" for BPM and "you" for the reader. Use "BPM" or "Belgarum Property Management" where the name helps, and in page titles.
- **Company name:** "Belgarum Property Management" on first use on a page, then "BPM" or "we". Do not use "BPM Ltd" in body copy.
- **Sentences:** aim for 20 words or fewer on average. One idea per sentence.
- **Paragraphs:** 1 to 3 sentences.
- **Voice:** active. "We send the budget in March", not "The budget is sent in March".
- **Contractions:** use them in general copy (we'll, you're, don't). Avoid them in legal statements and the complaints procedure.
- **Headings:** sentence case in the source ("How Section 20 works", not "How Section 20 Works"). Headings say what the section contains. No puns.
- **Lists:** use them for 3 or more parallel items. Start each item with a capital letter. No full stop unless an item is a full sentence.
- **Dashes:** do not use em dashes or en dashes. Use a comma, colon, brackets or a new sentence. Hyphens in compound words are fine.
- **Exclamation marks:** none.
- **Oxford comma:** no, unless it prevents a misreading.

### Numbers, money and dates

- Numerals for all numbers in body copy, except at the start of a sentence: "4 flats", "22 floors".
- Money: "£250", "£5 million", "£1,750". No ".00".
- Dates: "28 March 2026". Days and times: "Monday to Thursday, 9am to 5pm; Friday, 9am to 4.30pm".
- Ranges: "9am to 5pm", "3 to 5 days". Do not use a dash.
- Phone: "0800 298 0069".

### Terms and abbreviations

- Spell out an abbreviation the first time it appears on a page, then use the short form: "resident management company (RMC)". Meta titles and descriptions may use the short form.
- Do not spell out well-known terms (VAT, UK).
- Use the approved terms in `glossary.md`. Use one term for one thing.

### Links and calls to action

- Link text says where the link goes: "read our complaints procedure", not "click here".
- Calls to action start with a verb and say what happens:

These are the only call to action labels. `page-map.md` uses them. A page whose call to action is for leaseholders, but which buyers also read (Section 20 consultation, repairs and maintenance), may add one in-text link labelled "request a management proposal". The header and mobile menu buttons use "Get in touch" (to `/contact`, with no preset) and "Pay bill". The header phone link shows "Give us a call" above the number. Navigation links are not calls to action: menus and utility pages (404, confirmations) may use plain labels such as "Go to the home page" or "Try again".

| Use                                | Goes to                                            | Avoid           |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Get in touch                       | `/contact`, enquiry type preset to new management  | Enquire online  |
| Talk to us about a new development | `/contact`, enquiry type preset to new development | Find out more   |
| Pay your service charge            | `/pay-online`                                      |                 |
| Contact your property manager      | `/contact`                                         | Report a repair |

| Read our complaints procedure      | `/complaints-procedure`                            | Click here      |

| Call 0800 298 0069                 | `tel:` link                                        |                 |

## Words to use and avoid

| Use                      | Avoid                                        |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| help, support            | facilitate, assist                           |
| about                    | regarding, in relation to                    |
| start                    | commence                                     |
| use                      | utilise                                      |
| before                   | prior to                                     |
| check                    | ascertain                                    |
| we will                  | we shall                                     |
| block of flats, building | asset (except for freeholders and investors) |
| your property manager    | your account handler                         |
| leaseholder              | tenant (unless the reader rents)             |
| service charge           | maintenance fee                              |

Do not use: solutions, one-stop shop, bespoke, world-class, unrivalled, cutting-edge, seamless, peace of mind (as a claim), hassle-free, passionate, journey, synergy, leverage, best-in-class, award-winning (no award), leading.

## Checklist before publishing

1. Does the first sentence give the reader the main point?
2. Does every claim match a fact in `facts.md`?
3. Is every legal term the main reader may not know explained on first use?
4. Is any sentence over 25 words? Split it.
5. Is there any word from the "avoid" list?
6. Are there any em dashes, en dashes or exclamation marks?
7. Does every call to action start with a verb?
8. Read it aloud. Would a property manager say it to a client this way?

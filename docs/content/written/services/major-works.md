Major works

## description

## intro

### intro.heading

Planning large projects before they are needed

### intro.paragraphs

Major works are large, one-off projects such as a new roof, external redecoration or a lift renewal. They are often the biggest cost a building faces, and a common worry for leaseholders.

Belgarum Property Management plans these projects with whoever manages the building under the leases. That can be the freeholder (the owner of the building and the land), a resident management company (RMC) or a right to manage (RTM) company. We build them into the reserve fund, consult leaseholders as the law requires, and tender the work to contractors. Saving ahead means a smaller bill when works are due.

## included

### included.heading

What's included

### included.items

- Agreeing the scope of each project with the board or freeholder

## steps

### 1. Plan ahead

Your property manager inspects the building regularly and sees its condition. We use what we find to plan future works and the reserve fund. The reserve fund is money collected and saved for large costs.

### 2. Agree the scope

We agree with the board or freeholder what the project covers and when it should happen. Then we prepare it for consultation and tender.

### 3. Consult leaseholders

If any one leaseholder will pay more than £250, we run [Section 20 consultation](/services/section-20-consultation). Leaseholders are told about the works, can comment and can nominate a contractor.

### 4. Tender and appoint

We get estimates from contractors and report them to the board or freeholder. They choose the contractor, taking leaseholders' comments into account.

### 5. Carry out and pay for the works

The contractor carries out the works. The cost comes from the reserve fund, from a charge to leaseholders, or from both, in the way the lease sets out.

## faqs

### What counts as major works?

Major works are large, one-off projects. Common examples are roof replacement, external redecoration, window replacement and lift renewal. Day-to-day jobs, such as fixing a light or a door, are [repairs and maintenance](/services/repairs-and-maintenance).

### Why is my share of the major works so high?

Large projects cost a lot, and the cost is shared between the leaseholders. Your lease sets out your share. If the reserve fund doesn't cover the full cost, the rest is usually charged to leaseholders.

You can ask your property manager how the cost was set and how it is split. The Section 20 notices also show the estimates the cost is based on.

### When do I pay for major works?

That depends on your lease. Some or all of the cost may come from the reserve fund. Any extra is usually charged to leaseholders, in the shares the lease sets out. Your property manager can tell you how the cost will be met for your building.

### Can the reserve fund pay for everything?

Sometimes. It depends on how much has been saved and what the works cost. A well-planned reserve fund spreads the cost over several years, so the charge at the time of the works is smaller. Some leases don't allow a reserve fund, so check yours or ask your property manager.

### What are the CDM regulations?

The Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015, known as CDM, set health and safety duties for building work. They cover how work is planned, managed and carried out, including on blocks of flats. We take them into account when we plan works.

### Do leaseholders have a say in major works?

Yes. Under Section 20 consultation, leaseholders are told about the works, can comment on them and can nominate a contractor. The board or freeholder must take the comments into account before choosing a contractor. Read how [Section 20 consultation](/services/section-20-consultation) works.

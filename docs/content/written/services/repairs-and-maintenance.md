Repairs and maintenance

## description

How to report a repair in a block we manage, and who pays for what under the lease. We arrange the work with contractors we tender regularly.

## intro

### intro.heading

How to report a repair

### intro.paragraphs

If you live in a building we manage, report repairs to your property manager by phone or email. They deal with repairs to the communal areas: the shared parts of a building or estate, such as halls, stairs, roofs and gardens.

Belgarum Property Management arranges day-to-day repairs and maintenance for every building we manage. We use contractors from our own database and tender work regularly. Specialist work, such as lifts or asbestos, goes to specialist firms.

## included

### included.heading

What's included

### included.items

- Repairs to the communal areas, and to the other parts of the building that the lease makes the freeholder, RMC or RTM company repair
- A database of contractors, with work tendered regularly
- Specialist firms for lifts, asbestos and fire risk
- Regular site inspections, so your property manager can spot problems in the communal areas
- Paying for the work from the service charge, as the budget sets out

## steps

### 1. Tell your property manager

Contact your property manager by phone or email. Tell them the address, what the problem is, where it is and when it started. Photos help. `[NEEDS: repair reporting (Q7)]`

### 2. We check who is responsible

The lease decides who repairs what. We check whether the repair is for the building to pay for, or for the owner of a flat.

### 3. We arrange the work

If the repair is the building's responsibility, we instruct a contractor from our database. Larger or specialist jobs may need quotes first.

### 4. We pay the contractor

We pay the contractor's invoice from the building's service charge money. The cost shows in the year end service charge accounts.

## faqs

### Who is responsible for repairs inside my flat?

Your lease sets this out. Usually, the leaseholder repairs the inside of their own flat, such as the kitchen, bathroom, internal doors and their own pipes and wiring. Whoever manages the building usually repairs the structure, the roof, the external walls and the communal areas. That can be the freeholder (the owner of the building and the land), a resident management company (RMC) or a right to manage (RTM) company.

If you're not sure, check your lease or contact your property manager.

### How do I report a repair?

Contact your property manager by phone on 0800 298 0069 or by email at management@bpmanagement.org. Our office is open Monday to Thursday, 9am to 5pm, and Friday, 9am to 4.30pm.

If you rent your flat, report repairs inside the flat to your landlord or letting agent.

### What should I do in an emergency?

If there is a risk to life, such as a fire, call 999. If you smell gas, call the National Gas Emergency number on 0800 111 999.

`[NEEDS: out-of-hours line (Q2)]`

### Water is leaking into my flat. What should I do?

If the water is coming from the flat above, contact the people who live there first, as the leak is often inside their flat. If the leak comes from the roof, a communal pipe or a communal area, contact your property manager. If water is near electrics, turn off the electricity at the consumer unit if it is safe to do so.

### Who pays for repairs to the building?

Repairs that the lease makes the building's responsibility are paid from the service charge. The service charge is the money leaseholders pay, under their lease, towards running, repairing and insuring the building. Large, one-off projects are [major works](/services/major-works), and the law may require leaseholders to be consulted first.

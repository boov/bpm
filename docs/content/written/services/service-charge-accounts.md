## title

Service charge accounts

## description

Service charge collection, arrears and year end accounts from our own accounts team, with each building's money in its own designated trust account.

Hero call to action: Get in touch

## intro

### intro.heading

Keeping the building's money in order

### intro.paragraphs

Every block and estate needs someone to collect the service charge, pay the bills and chase late payments. At the year end, someone must show where the money went. The service charge is the money leaseholders pay, under their lease, towards running, repairing and insuring the building.

Belgarum Property Management does this with its own accounts team. Each building's money is held in a designated trust account. This is a bank account the law says is held on trust for the leaseholders, separate from our own money.

## included

### included.heading

What's included

### included.items

- Bookkeeping for each block or estate through the year
- Sending service charge demands and collecting payments
- Credit control by our own team when payments are late
- Arrears recovery through independent solicitors, when reminders don't work
- Paying contractors and suppliers from the building's trust account
- Year end service charge accounts, comparing what was spent with the budget
- Balancing charges or credits for each leaseholder after the year end

## steps

### 1. Demands go out

We send each leaseholder a service charge demand for their share of the budget, as the lease sets out.

### 2. Payments come in

Payments go into the designated trust account for the block or estate. Leaseholders can pay online by card.

### 3. Late payments are chased

Our credit control team contacts leaseholders whose payments are late. If the arrears are still not paid, we can pass the case to independent solicitors.

### 4. Every cost is recorded

Our accounts team records each bill paid from the building's money, against the budget line it belongs to.

### 5. Accounts at the year end

We prepare the service charge accounts, which compare actual spending with the budget. Any difference becomes a balancing charge or a balancing credit for each leaseholder.

## faqs

### What are service charge accounts?

Service charge accounts are the year end statement of what was actually spent on the building, compared with the budget. They show whether leaseholders paid too much or too little during the year.

### What is a balancing charge?

A balancing charge is the extra amount a leaseholder pays if the year's spending was more than the budget. If spending was less, the leaseholder gets a balancing credit instead. Your share is set by your lease.

### What happens if a leaseholder doesn't pay their service charge?

Our credit control team contacts them first. If the arrears are still not paid, the case can go to independent solicitors. Depending on the lease, the costs of recovering arrears may be charged to the leaseholder who owes them.

Unpaid service charges affect everyone in the building, because the budget depends on every share being paid.

### Can I see the invoices behind the accounts?

Leaseholders can ask in writing for a summary of the costs behind their service charge. Within 6 months of getting the summary, they can ask in writing to inspect the invoices and receipts. Ask your property manager.

### How do I pay my service charge?

You can [pay your service charge](/pay-online) online by debit or credit card. Use the amount on your demand. If you have a question about your demand, contact your property manager.

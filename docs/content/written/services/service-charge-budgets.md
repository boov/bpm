## intro

### intro.heading

What a service charge budget does

### intro.paragraphs

The service charge is the money leaseholders pay, under their lease, towards running, repairing and insuring the building. The service charge budget is the plan for the year's spending. It sets how much each leaseholder pays.

Belgarum Property Management prepares the budget with whoever manages the building under the leases. That can be the freeholder (the owner of the building and the land), a resident management company (RMC) or a right to manage (RTM) company. A sound budget covers the year's costs and saves for large ones. That way, leaseholders are less likely to face sudden bills.

## included

What's included

## steps

### 1. Look back at the last year

We compare last year's spending with its budget. This shows where costs went up or down, and why.

### 2. Draft the new budget

We set out the costs for the coming year, such as insurance, cleaning, gardening, repairs and our management fee. We add what the building needs to save in the reserve fund.

### 3. Agree it with the board or freeholder

We go through the draft with the board or freeholder. They agree the final budget.

### 4. Send the demands

We work out each leaseholder's share, as the lease sets out, and send the service charge demands. The money goes into the building's designated trust account.

### 5. Track spending through the year

Our accounts team records every cost against the budget. At the year end, this becomes the [service charge accounts](/services/service-charge-accounts).

## faqs

### Why has my service charge gone up?

Usually because the building's costs have risen, or it needs to save more for future works. Insurance, energy, contracts and repairs are common causes.

Each budget line shows what the money is for, and your property manager can take you through it.

### What is a reserve fund?

A reserve fund is money collected and saved for large future costs, such as a new roof or external redecoration. It is sometimes called a sinking fund. Saving a little each year means a smaller bill when the work is due. Your lease sets out whether the building can hold a reserve fund.

### What does the service charge pay for?

It pays for running, repairing and insuring the building, as the lease sets out. Typical costs include insurance, cleaning, gardening, repairs and the management fee. It may also include a payment into the reserve fund.

### Who decides the budget?

The freeholder, RMC or RTM company that manages the building under the leases. We prepare the budget and advise on it. The board or freeholder agrees it.

### What can I do if I think a service charge is unreasonable?

The law says service charges must be reasonable. Start by asking your property manager to explain the charge. If you still disagree, you can take independent advice. A tribunal can decide whether a service charge is reasonable.

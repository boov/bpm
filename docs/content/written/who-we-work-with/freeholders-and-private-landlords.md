## intro

### intro.heading

Meeting your obligations as a freeholder

### intro.paragraphs

As a freeholder, you have obligations under the leases and the law. You must usually repair and insure the building, consult leaseholders before major works and hold their service charge money on trust. The service charge is what leaseholders pay, under their lease, towards running, repairing and insuring the building.

Belgarum Property Management does that work for freeholders, and for private landlords who own a small block or a house split into flats. We manage buildings from 4 flats upwards. We don't sell or let property.

## faqs

### What legal obligations does a freeholder have?

They depend on the leases and the building. Most freeholders must repair and insure the building, and consult leaseholders before major works or long-term contracts. Service charge money must be held on trust. The freeholder is also usually the "responsible person" for fire safety in the communal areas. For advice on your own position, take independent legal advice.

### Do you find tenants or let flats?

No. We manage the building and its communal areas. We don't sell or let property. If you let flats in the building, you can use a letting agent or manage the tenancies yourself.

### How do you look after the building's condition?

Your property manager inspects the building and communal areas regularly. We arrange repairs through contractors from our database, and plan major works and the reserve fund ahead. This keeps the building in good order for the leaseholders and for you.

### How do you collect arrears?

Our own credit control team contacts leaseholders whose payments are late. If the arrears are still not paid, the case can go to independent solicitors.

### How will you keep me informed?

You have one dedicated property manager, available Monday to Thursday, 9am to 5pm, and Friday, 9am to 4.30pm. Your property manager inspects the building regularly, and larger developments get regular management reports.

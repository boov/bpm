## intro

### intro.heading

You give your time. We carry the workload.

### intro.paragraphs

As a director of a resident management company (RMC) or a right to manage (RTM) company, you're usually a volunteer leaseholder. You give your time for free, but you carry legal duties: company law, fire safety, health and safety and the terms of the leases.

Belgarum Property Management is a managing agent: a firm the board employs to run the building day to day. We do the work that helps you meet your duties. Your board keeps the decisions, and you get one dedicated property manager as your contact.

## faqs

### What is the difference between an RMC and an RTM company?

An RMC is a company, usually owned by the leaseholders, that is responsible under the leases for managing the building or estate.

An RTM company is set up by leaseholders to take over the management of their building from the freeholder, using a legal right. The freeholder still owns the building.

### Do directors keep their legal duties if they appoint a managing agent?

Yes. Directors keep their duties under the Companies Act 2006, and the company keeps its duties under the leases and safety law. A managing agent does the work that helps the board meet those duties, and has duties of its own for the work its contract covers. For advice on your own position, take independent legal advice.

### How do we move to BPM from our current managing agent?

Check your current management agreement for its notice period. The board then decides when to give notice. `[NEEDS: switching process (Q8)]`

To compare us with your current agent, [request a management proposal](/contact?type=new-management). It sets out what we would do for your building.

### We're thinking about the right to manage. Can you help?

We manage buildings for RTM companies once they have taken over management. Since 3 March 2025, the RTM company and its members do not usually pay the freeholder's costs of a claim. A tribunal can still order costs in some cases, such as a claim withdrawn unreasonably. An RTM claim is also possible where up to 50% of the building is not residential. For advice on making a claim, take independent legal advice.

### What does the board still decide?

The big decisions stay with the board. These include agreeing the service charge budget, choosing contractors for major works, and deciding how to enforce the leases. We give you the information and advice to make those decisions.
